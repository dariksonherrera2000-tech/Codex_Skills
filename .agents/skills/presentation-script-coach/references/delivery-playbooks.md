# Delivery Playbooks

Use this reference to choose the speaking posture, pacing, proof style, and Q&A defense for PPT/slides. Adapt to the user's industry and audience. Do not invent facts.

## Quick Selection

| Scenario | Talk wins when | Speaking posture | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales proposal | Customer believes the value is urgent and credible | Consultative, customer-first | Feature dumping, vendor bragging |
| Investor pitch | Investor wants a follow-up meeting | Sharp, opportunity-led | Over-explaining product details |
| Promotion defense | Reviewers see next-level scope and judgment | Evidence-backed, reflective | Listing tasks without abstraction |
| Quarterly/annual report | Decision-maker sees progress, risks, and next asks | Executive, concise | Activity logs and unsupported optimism |
| Technical talk | Peers learn a transferable idea | Precise, candid | Hiding limitations or unreadable details |
| Training | Learners can apply the skill | Patient, example-led | Long theory without practice |
| Consulting delivery | Client trusts the recommendation and next steps | Structured, decision-oriented | Ambiguous ownership or caveats buried late |
| Retrospective | Organization learns what to repeat or change | Honest, non-blaming | Vague root causes |
| Case study | Audience believes the result can translate | Story-led, concrete | Anonymous claims without context |

## Sales Proposal / Customer Solution

Audience asks: "Why now, why you, and how do I reduce risk?"

Script strategy:
- Open with the customer's pain or decision pressure, not your company profile.
- Translate every capability into customer impact.
- Use "you/your team/your current process" more than "we/our platform".
- Slow down on ROI, implementation path, security, migration, and customer case pages.
- Prepare answers for price, proof, timeline, competitor comparison, and risk.

Useful phrasing:
- "The reason this matters for your team is..."
- "I don't want to overstate this number; the current evidence shows..."
- "If we de-risk this as a pilot, the first milestone should be..."

## Investor Pitch

Audience asks: "Is this a big opportunity, and is this the team to win it?"

Script strategy:
- In the first minute, state problem, solution, and why timing matters.
- Keep product explanation short; use traction and market logic to carry conviction.
- When numbers are early, say what is observed vs assumed.
- Slow down on market, traction, business model, and ask.
- Prepare answers for TAM/SAM/SOM, growth quality, retention, CAC, moat, and use of funds.

Useful phrasing:
- "The wedge is narrow by design; the expansion path is..."
- "What we have validated so far is..."
- "The next financing milestone is not vanity growth; it is..."

## Promotion Defense

Audience asks: "Are you already operating at the next level?"

Script strategy:
- Tie outcomes to scope, difficulty, judgment, and repeatable method.
- Use "I" for accountability and "we" for team execution; avoid hiding personal contribution.
- Abstract from project details into transferable operating principles.
- Slow down on business understanding, hardest project, method, and future scope.
- Prepare answers for ownership, tradeoffs, failures, team influence, and next-level expectations.

Useful phrasing:
- "The hard part was not execution volume; it was the judgment call..."
- "What I learned and can now repeat is..."
- "At the next level, I would expand this from... to..."

## Quarterly / Annual Report

Audience asks: "What changed, why, what risks remain, and what decision is needed?"

Script strategy:
- Lead with the executive answer before details.
- For every metric, name the baseline: target, last period, forecast, peer, or customer expectation.
- Separate results, causes, risks, and asks.
- Slow down on misses, resource requests, and next-quarter plan.
- Prepare answers for data口径, misses, resource tradeoffs, and risk controls.

Useful phrasing:
- "The headline is..."
- "Compared with the target, this means..."
- "The decision I need today is..."

## Technical Talk

Audience asks: "What can I learn or reuse?"

Script strategy:
- State the pain and constraints before architecture.
- Explain one idea at a time; let diagrams do structure, not decoration.
- Be explicit about tradeoffs and limitations.
- Slow down on the problem framing, architecture, benchmark, demo, and failure modes.
- Prepare answers for alternatives, scalability, security, performance, and operational cost.

Useful phrasing:
- "The constraint that shaped the design was..."
- "This works well when..., but breaks down when..."
- "The transferable pattern is..."

## Training

Audience asks: "Can I do this myself after the session?"

Script strategy:
- Move from concept to example to practice.
- Use repetition and signposting.
- Add micro-checks: "pause here, can you identify..."
- Slow down on instructions and exercises.
- Prepare answers for beginner confusion, setup issues, and common mistakes.

Useful phrasing:
- "The easiest way to remember this is..."
- "Let's try it once together, then you do it..."
- "If you only remember one thing from this section..."

## Consulting / Strategy Delivery

Audience asks: "Do we trust this recommendation, and what do we do next?"

Script strategy:
- Use issue-tree logic: answer first, evidence second, action third.
- Distinguish facts, interpretation, and recommendation.
- Make ownership and next steps concrete.
- Slow down on recommendation, decision options, tradeoffs, and implementation roadmap.
- Prepare answers for assumptions, sensitivity, stakeholder impact, and execution risk.

Useful phrasing:
- "Our recommendation is..."
- "This conclusion depends on three assumptions..."
- "The practical implication for the next 30 days is..."

## Retrospective

Audience asks: "What should we repeat or change?"

Script strategy:
- Keep tone accountable and non-blaming.
- Compare expected vs actual.
- Name root causes, not symptoms.
- Slow down on lessons and changed operating rules.
- Prepare answers for ownership, prevention, and follow-through.

Useful phrasing:
- "The pattern underneath this incident is..."
- "The behavior we should keep is..."
- "The system change that prevents a repeat is..."

## Case Study

Audience asks: "Is this real, and could it apply to us?"

Script strategy:
- Tell the case as before, intervention, result, lesson.
- Use customer context, constraints, and caveats.
- Avoid over-generalizing one case.
- Slow down on baseline and result.
- Prepare answers for comparability, source, and what would differ elsewhere.

Useful phrasing:
- "This case is relevant because..."
- "The result should be read with one caveat..."
- "What transfers is not the exact number, but the mechanism..."
